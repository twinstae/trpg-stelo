How blessed, how very blessed we are to call a place like Neandran home. It's a cold, dangerous world that shows no mercy to the gentle, the careful, or the wise, let alone the young, the weak, or the brash. But safely enclosed by the arms of Neandran, deep within the hidden crags and lush forests of the Glimmering Valley, we are cared for, protected, and nurtured as well as any could hope in this world.

You look around and know that the world is very old. Even the valley itself was shaped by people who lived long ago. You can see their handiwork here and there in the valley's walls, in some of the nearby empty ruins, or in the temple at the very heart of Neandran. There are those in the village that believe the ancient creators of such things were not people, but rather demons of ancient days. The loudest voice behind such thoughts is Easchel, but we'll get to the specifics of the village's colorful folk soon enough.

## The Dream

Everyone in Neandran has the same dream each night, without exception. It's the only dream we've ever known (and in fact the very concept that someone might have a different dream is unknown to us). Some people even occasionally experience the dream as a waking vision as well as when sleeping. It's always there, and in a way it ties us all together.

Now, I cannot truthfully tell you what the dream is, or what it is about, or even describe it to an outsider. I don't have the words to do so and they don't have the words to understand me if I did.

The dream swims with images, shapes, and even colors you won't see anywhere else. Over the years, folk have come up with names for some of them. There's the color *vell*, a creature they call the toothed eye, a structure named the growing forest of steel, and a sound called the wailing of the wood. But these names don't really help to explain or understand.

The important thing to know is that we have always had the dream. And less than two weeks ago, it began to change. The images shifted. There were new sights and sounds, but all just as inexplicable as before. Everyone's talking about it but no one has any explanations.

## The Village

You've lived in Neandran your whole life. There's no one among its four hundred and some inhabitants that you haven't met and likely spoken to many times. Likewise, we all know you. We remember that foolish thing you did when you were younger, but also the achievements you've accomplished as well. We know your family, and we know your friends. We are your family and friends.

While technically the folk of the village utilize a barter system, the truth is Luttan makes sure that everyone has what they need when they need it. Shins are used only to deal with the occasional trader that comes to the village from far-off Ketterach, bringing things the village doesn't provide on its own, like metal, sugar, spices, and more. Concepts like individualized wealth or social class have no meaning in Neandran.

### Places in the Village

![Map of the village of Neandran](/glimmering-valley-neandran-map.webp)

At the very center of Neandran stands the temple. It's an ancient structure, and some claim it wasn't even built by human hands. Sesson, the village priest, holds rites and ceremonies in honor of Loe.

If you're looking for someone once the workday is done, the Post is likely where you should start your search. It's a gathering place where all are welcome, and a friendly fellow named Bik serves food and drink for all that wish it.

The crystal monoliths stand equidistant from the Temple, and seem likely to be just as old. Everyone knows that they give off some kind of energy or influence and cause strange things to happen. Children born in the village sometimes possess physical mutations, while a few others have abilities that most folk here call sorcery. And that's a bit frightening. Still, the wisest of us also know that this same essence makes our farmland so fertile and our flocks so healthy.

The village has multiple storehouses of food and supplies. Luttan is in charge of all of them and she keeps a careful tally of all the village's stores.

While Neandran boasts woodcutters, hunters, fisherfolk, and many other roles, none are as important or as numerous as the farmers and herders. Folks work long hours in the fields growing thates. These tubers can be cooked in any of a number of ways, or even ground to make flour for bread. Other folks tend to the flocks of gobrin—shaggy grey goat-like creatures—that supply us milk, wool, and sometimes meat. Don't be surprised if you see one with more legs than it should have, or even two heads. That's just the way we grow them here in the valley.

Some of the villagers' pet seskii help with the gobrin herds, but not all.

Many sacks of thates are toted down to the river where they're ground into flour in the mill, upriver from the fishing-boat docks.

## The Valley

The valley is many miles long, and its walls are high and extremely steep. If you look closely, you see lines here and there on those walls that are too straight or regular to be simply stone and earth. We know that long before we were here, ancient peoples built the structures that now compose our beloved valley's walls. Sesson tells us that they did so with the help and instruction of Loe, and it is to Them that we owe our gratitude for the valley and its peaceful shelter. Honestly, most of us don't think about it much. We're too busy tending our herds, planting our crops, and most importantly living our lives today, not in some unimaginable past.

The head of the valley is the northernmost point, where mountain streams pour themselves down in high waterfalls to gather and form the cold, clear, and deep Lake Memory. Fish in the lake are plentiful, as are indigo-colored leeches that can grow to be 6 inches (15 cm) in length. There are a few people who live around the lake, about a half hour's walk from the village. You've heard of the Oracle, Thalgre, for example, although you've yet to meet him. He uses ancient magic to foretell the future.

Loe's River flows from the lake and winds its course through the lowest part of the valley, which is usually its center. It eventually flows out of the valley, past a distant city called Ketterach, but that's far away and not our concern.

The air in the valley is generally cool and still. The valley walls make for short days, blocking both the morning and evening sun. Seasons are regular and consistent, with cold rainy winters, moderately warm springs and falls, and warm—but rarely hot—summers.

Fisherfolk use nets in the river to catch silver coppos, while others seek out frogs and turtles for their eggs.

### People in the Woods

Very few people live on their own, away from the village. Two such individuals are siblings, named Belof and Echealli. These two ne'er-do-wells lead a small band of ruffians living somewhere near the river. They're from Neandran originally but left after causing a lot of trouble. Life in the woods hasn't made them any better, and they're always getting up to something untoward.

Farther out still is the woman we just call the Old Witch. She brews potions and other magical concoctions, and folks in need of her help can get it if they've a stout heart and something to trade. By some accounts, the witch is as old as the valley itself, and even if that's hard to believe, you can be sure that your grandparents and even their grandparents remember her being out there in her strange house she calls "the Infinite Abode." Kids say that her house looks like it's made out of some sort of bread, but that can't be true. Can it?

It's worth noting that in the mid to late autumn, a nomadic band of nonhuman folk called the briinii come to the valley for the winter. They make a camp for months, but it is miles from Neandran. You've only heard of them—you've never seen one. Some villagers say it's best to steer clear of them because they're so different from "normal people."

### Plants and Animals in the Valley

Soldier oaks and prickly luva, each entwined in balla vines, keep the forest thick and green, with tall zurain adding a touch of blue. If you need a tent for the night, look for the massive erien trees with their gigantic leaves—those leaves make fine boats as well.

You'll see both red- and blue-feathered voot in the trees, as well as grief birds, known for their wailing song. The insectoid birds called baroon hunt tiny tree-dwelling hornhogs and burrowing nobba, but hunters truly prize the graceful two-legged rean.

Beware the sarrata, whose spittle will make you see wondrous things—right before it kills you. Almost as bad are the swarms of veeshin snakes. But perhaps as you make your way through the wood, you'll see one of the enormous chichachi, glowing worms whose appearance is a good omen.

### Valley Locations

![Map of the Glimmering Valley](/glimmering-valley-map.webp)

At the other end of the valley lies Ketterach. You've never been there, but you've heard tell of its wonders. Its population is tenfold that of Neandran, and its soldiers ride the winds on artificial wings. The people there, it is said, know much more about the wonders of the past, and even more about the world beyond the valley.

If you're an adventurous sort, you've probably gone deep enough into the forest to see the River Ring. Near the remnants of an old machine, in a shallow pond fed by the river, the water rises up and forms a large, floating triangle. Why do we call it the River Ring? While not an ancient mystery like the formation itself, no one alive knows the origin of the name for certain, but most assume that in generations past, the water formed a circle rather than a triangle.

Likewise, far from the village, the Dreaming Cubes wait in a glade, as it seems they have since before the village existed. These large blue cubes rest atop glistening metal pedestals like tree stumps. Strangest of all, however, is that if one gets close, the hazy form of a living creature can be seen within the cube, like a fly suspended in amber. Each creature is different, and none are recognizable as a creature alive today, at least around here.

Closer to home, the entrance to the Empty Caves can be found in the valley wall. This is an extensive subterranean catacomb, and to be honest, they're probably not truly caves at all, but ancient chambers of some kind with metal walls and strange things within. In your grandmother's time, people still went into these caves—called by some other name then—and came out with trinkets and useful oddities, or even some useful materials, but those days are long gone. The only thing you'll find there today are ghosts, if you believe in that sort of thing.

## Names

People of Neandran don't use surnames. A single, typically one- or two-syllable name is most common, usually with moderately soft sounds like Amrose or Luttan. The only person with a hard K in their name is Kyath, and he's not originally from the village. Names usually end in a consonant sound.

Names are not tied to a gender or status.

Names usually cycle through the generations in a family, but never while the original name holder is alive. So Thear had a grandfather named Thear, and her parents named her because he had already passed by the time she was born. Her family won't name a child Thear again until she has, in turn, passed. Reusing the name of an ancestor is always meant to be a way of honoring the past.

While you can choose any single name you wish, try to make it fit with the others in the village.

## Families and Friends

All PCs have family and friends in Neandran. Working with the GM, you should choose at least one and perhaps as many as five or six NPCs in the village. The PC is either related to or particularly friendly with these characters, perhaps even living in the same house as they do.

The village's population far exceeds the named NPCs, however, so there is ample room for new characters to be added as needed. For example, a character might know Veri and Yurran as they grew up right next door, and thus by extension they might know Amrose and Aryle (Veri's parents) fairly well, but your character's actual parents are NPCs of your creation.

Family and friends offer a way to explain the equipment and cyphers you start with, and perhaps the training you have in your skills and abilities. Encourage both comparing and contrasting NPCs with the PCs. That is to say, a PC who is friends with Luttan might be very well organized, smart, and devoted to the village, but they might also be friends with Mert, a skilled hunter and tracker, and also a bit of a troublemaker in town. Our friends sometimes complement our outlook, but they sometimes also provide interesting foils. Imagine the awkward situation where a PC who Bears a Halo of Fire lives next door to Easchel. Or perhaps Easchel is their father!

## Village Connections

Above and beyond knowing who their friends and family in Neandran might be, each PC should have a special link to one of the named NPCs. Pick a person in the village to have this strong connection to your character. Provide a narrative reason for the connection that ties in with your character's background.

Once per day (once between ten-hour rests), you can make the equivalent of a player intrusion but with no XP cost. However, the intrusion must relate to the connected NPC from the village.

For example:

- [Connected NPC] has the tool I need and happens to walk by so I can ask them for it.
- [Connected NPC] knows the cure for this malady and will share it with me.
- [Connected NPC] will hide us for the evening in their house.

The connection, of course, has the most value when players are in the village. In the valley outside the village, the connection can be called upon in less direct ways. Such intrusions might be linked to advice, gifts, or tiny bits of knowledge that happen to be useful in a given situation. Examples:

- [Connected NPC] told me about a plant that grows in deep gullies that might treat this poison.
- [Connected NPC] once mentioned these beasts and that they have an aversion to water.
- [Connected NPC] gave me this tool we need before we left the village.
- [Connected NPC] once showed me a trick to fix a broken bow like this.

Obviously, the intrusion has to fit the NPC as well as the situation. Ien could have shared insight about the best place to ford the river, but Easchel, who has never left Neandran, couldn't. Likewise, Alloise is unlikely to have had a rock-crafting tool to give the PC, but she might have given them something more suited to leatherworking.
